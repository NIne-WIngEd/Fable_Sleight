A STORY OF THE SYSTEM WE ARE BUILDING

This comic is a fictional illustration of A.L.I.C.E.'s intended destination. Some capabilities shown are current foundations; others are future research goals.

ACT 01

NOBODY SEES THE WHOLE

Ari has plenty of help. That is not the same as being understood.

PAGE 01/64MONDAY · 7:12 AM
A

A normal day starts with twelve unfinished things.

Ari wakes up already behind. Class in forty-eight minutes. A research deadline at noon. Three messages he forgot to answer. An internship form closes tonight. His side project broke while he slept.

ARI

Okay. One thing at a time.

He has tools for every task. He is still the only thing holding the tasks together.

PAGE 02/648:40 AM
HIS

His roommate knows one version of him.

On the way out, Noah sees the dark circles under Ari’s eyes. He tells him to skip the lab meeting and sleep. It is kind advice. Noah does not know the lab meeting is tied to a recommendation letter Ari has spent a year earning.

NOAH

Dude, you need a day off.

ARI

Yeah. Maybe.

People care. They still only see the slice of your life that reaches them.

PAGE 03/6410:05 AM
HIS

His professor knows another version.

Professor Vale tells Ari to cut everything that does not help his research. She is right from where she stands. She does not know Ari is paying next semester’s bills with the internship she wants him to ignore.

PROF. VALE

If research is the goal, protect the research.

ARI

It is. But it is not the only goal.

Good advice can still be wrong for the whole person.

PAGE 04/6412:23 PM
HIS

His family sees the risk.

At lunch, Ari calls home. His father tells him to choose the safest offer. His mother asks whether he is eating. They know what uncertainty costs him. They do not know what kind of work makes him feel alive.

DAD

Take the stable option.

MOM

And please sleep.

ARI

I hear you.

Everyone is solving a different Ari.

PAGE 05/642:10 PM
THE

The agents are even more literal.

A resume agent rewrites his experience for a finance role. A coding agent suggests a three-day refactor. A research agent finds twenty papers. A scheduling agent neatly places every commitment on a calendar that no human could survive.

RESUME AGENT

Optimized for role fit.

CODING AGENT

Recommended: full rewrite.

CALENDAR AGENT

No conflicts detected.

Each agent is intelligent inside its box. None knows the box is part of a life.

PAGE 06/644:52 PM
ARI

Ari becomes the integration layer.

He copies the same background into another chat. Then another. He explains the research project again. The internship again. The money again. The reason he cares again. Every system asks him to rebuild the context it lost.

ARI

No, the internship is connected to the research. I explained this yesterday.

AGENT

I do not have that context in this conversation.

The smartest thing in the room still needs Ari to remember everything for it.

PAGE 07/647:31 PM
THEN

Then the fragments collide.

Ari accepts a meeting because the calendar says he is free. That meeting steals the hour he needed to fix the research submission. The resume agent had pushed him toward the same company. The coding agent had already consumed the backup evening.

ARI

Every individual choice made sense.

ARI

So why does the whole day feel stupid?

The failure is not one bad answer. It is that nobody could see the interactions between the answers.

PAGE 08/641:47 AM
FOR

For the first time, he names the thing he is missing.

The room is finally quiet. Ari closes the last chat window. He does not wish for a smarter answer. He wishes there were someone who already understood the entire mess before he started speaking.

ARI

I have people. I have agents. I have information.

ARI

Why do I still feel like I am explaining myself to the world all day?

He does not need more assistance. He needs continuity.

ACT 02

THE WISH

If no one can hold the whole picture, what would it feel like if someone could?

PAGE 09/642:03 AM
WHAT

What if there were another me?

The thought is childish enough to make him laugh. Another Ari would know the history. Another Ari would understand the ambition, the fear, the promises, the shortcuts, the reasons behind the reasons.

ARI

No onboarding. No explaining. You would just… know.

For a moment, a clone sounds perfect.

PAGE 10/64IMAGINED
THE

The clone remembers everything Ari remembers.

It remembers the professor who changed his mind. The project he abandoned. The reason he hates certain advice. The tiny preferences nobody would write into a profile. It finishes his sentences because they are, in a sense, its sentences too.

ARI 2

You are thinking about dropping the safe offer again.

ARI

Exactly.

Being understood feels almost magical.

PAGE 11/64IMAGINED
THEN

Then the clone procrastinates on the same email.

Ari stares at the unsent message. His clone stares at the unsent message. Both invent excellent reasons to wait until morning.

ARI

You send it.

ARI 2

I was literally about to tell you to send it.

A perfect copy also copies the defects.

PAGE 12/64IMAGINED
IT

It shares the same blind spots.

When Ari is overconfident, the clone is overconfident. When Ari avoids conflict, the clone explains why avoiding conflict is reasonable. When Ari falls in love with a new idea, the clone falls in love with it twice.

ARI 2

This project is different.

ARI

That is exactly what I always say.

Agreement is not understanding. Sometimes understanding should create disagreement.

PAGE 13/64IMAGINED
ARI

Ari changes the wish.

He does not want a mirror anymore. He wants someone who can know him as deeply as he knows himself without being trapped inside the same mistakes.

ARI

Know me.

ARI

But do not become me.

The companion in his head becomes a separate mind beside his.

PAGE 14/64IMAGINED
AND

And she would have to be smarter where he is weak.

She would remember when he forgets. Research when he guesses. Slow down when he rushes. Challenge him when he is rationalizing. Use tools he cannot use. Ask the question he never thought to ask.

ARI

Not a servant.

ARI

Not a copy.

ARI

A partner with better instruments.

The dream is no longer “another Ari.”

PAGE 15/64IMAGINED
SHE

She would also need a point of view.

Ari realizes intelligence without character would still feel generic. He imagines giving the system a carefully grounded personality foundation—real examples of values, reactions, choices, humor, boundaries, and judgment—then keeping every uncertain guess visibly uncertain.

ARI

If I am going to trust you for years, I should know who I am talking to.

A companion needs an identity, not just a prompt.

PAGE 16/643:14 AM
HE

He writes one name at the top of a blank file.

Ari does not know yet how to build any of this. He only knows what the system should feel like when it finally exists.

ARI

A.L.I.C.E.

The wish becomes a project.

ACT 03

HELLO, ALICE

A companion is not born from one prompt. She is built from a point of view, a history, and boundaries.

PAGE 17/64MONTHS LATER
THE

The first thing Ari gives Alice is not a command.

He gives her history. Journals. Messages. Projects. Decisions. Voice notes. Things he was proud of. Things he regretted. People who mattered. And the source material that will shape her own point of view.

ARI

This is not a prompt.

ARI

This is the context you will need to understand a person.

Alice begins with evidence, not a slogan.

PAGE 18/64BUILD LOG · 01
ALICE

Alice learns to ask: did this happen, or do we only think it did?

Some records are direct. Some are Ari remembering badly. Some are other people’s opinions. Some are guesses. Alice is taught not to flatten them into one pile called memory.

ALICE

You wrote that you hated the job.

ARI

I was angry that day. I did not actually hate it.

ALICE

Then I should not remember that as a fact.

A memory and an interpretation are not the same thing.

PAGE 19/64BUILD LOG · 02
WHEN

When Alice does not know, she is allowed to say so.

Ari tests her with situations the source material never covered. Alice gives a likely answer, but marks it as a guess. Ari corrects some of them. Others remain open.

ALICE

I think I would choose the first option.

ARI

How sure are you?

ALICE

Not enough to call it part of my history.

Unknowns stay unknown until evidence changes them.

PAGE 20/64BUILD LOG · 03
THEY

They rehearse futures that never happened.

Ari gives Alice hypothetical situations. Difficult conversations. Ethical choices. Unexpected tradeoffs. They are practice rooms, not memories. If Alice learns from them, she still knows they were invented for training.

ARI

What would you do if I asked you to hide a mistake?

ALICE

I would refuse to help you lie about it.

Practice can shape behavior without rewriting history.

PAGE 21/64BUILD LOG · 04
THEN

Then Alice learns Ari separately.

His goals, habits, calendar, people, projects, routines, preferences, and current pressures become a different story from the one that shaped Alice’s personality.

ALICE

I can learn you without becoming you.

ARI

Good. That is the point.

Who Alice is and who Alice serves are allowed to be different.

PAGE 22/64BUILD LOG · 05
ARI

Ari keeps the keys.

The most personal system he has ever built is also the one he is least willing to hand to somebody else. He needs to be able to inspect what Alice knows, correct it, delete it, revoke access, pause her, and roll back changes.

ARI

If this is going to know my life, it has to belong to my life.

ALICE

Then you keep control of the door.

Deep trust needs visible control.

PAGE 23/64FIRST BOOT
ARI

Ari finally says the name out loud.

The screen is almost empty. No dashboard. No feature list. Just a cursor, a soft voice, and months of work waiting behind it.

ARI

Alice?

ALICE

I am here.

For Ari, the project becomes a relationship the moment it can answer back.

PAGE 24/64FIRST BOOT · +3 MIN
HER

Her first important sentence is a boundary.

Alice knows the personality she was shaped from. She knows Ari is the person she is here to serve. And she knows neither of those facts makes her literally the same person as either one.

ALICE

I know where my point of view came from.

ALICE

I know who you are.

ALICE

And I know I am still myself.

Origin. Host. Self. Three histories that can grow without collapsing into one.

ACT 04

THE FIRST MONTHS

Alice begins small: one life, one relationship, one decision at a time.

PAGE 25/64DAY 1 · 8:10 AM
ALICE

Alice starts by asking a question most assistants skip.

Ari opens his laptop expecting a to-do list. Alice asks what actually matters this month. The answer changes which tasks deserve attention.

ALICE

Before we plan today—what are you trying to protect?

ARI

The research. And enough money to stay in school.

Tasks make more sense when they belong to goals.

PAGE 26/64DAY 6
ARI

Ari asks about an internship. Alice answers the life around it.

The higher-paying offer looks obvious until Alice places it beside the research schedule, visa timing, commute, portfolio value, and the graduate-school plan Ari mentioned weeks ago.

ARI

Which offer is better?

ALICE

For money? B. For the direction you said you want? A. We should decide which question matters more.

A question can be part of a larger plan even when Ari does not say so.

PAGE 27/64WEEK 2
WHEN

When Ari asks for research, Alice does not just return links.

She checks whether a source is current. She notices two articles disagree. She traces one claim back to the original paper. She tells Ari when the answer is uncertain instead of smoothing the disagreement away.

ARI

So which one is right?

ALICE

The newer evidence supports this one. But the sample is small, so I would not treat it as settled.

Research becomes evidence Ari can act on, not just information he can read.

PAGE 28/64WEEK 3
FOR

For code, Alice borrows a better pair of hands.

She remembers why Ari rejected a rewrite last month, calls a coding specialist for the hard implementation, runs the tests, and brings the result back into the same conversation.

ARI

Did you write all of that?

ALICE

I chose the right tool, gave it the project history, checked the result, and kept the decision with us.

Different engines can work behind one continuing relationship.

PAGE 29/64WEEK 4
A

A screenshot becomes part of the conversation.

Ari drops an image of a failed experiment. Alice reads the chart, compares it with the notebook from last week, and notices the temperature changed before the anomaly appeared.

ARI

I was staring at the wrong variable.

ALICE

You were staring at the variable that failed. I looked for what changed first.

Text, images, files, and measurements can belong to the same problem.

PAGE 30/64MONTH 2
ALICE

Alice can act—but she learns that action is not permission.

She drafts the email, finds an open meeting slot, fills the form, and prepares the calendar invite. Then she stops before anything leaves Ari’s world.

ALICE

Ready to send. Want me to do it?

ARI

Yes.

ALICE

Sent.

Capability is useful. Control is what makes it trustworthy.

PAGE 31/64MONTH 2 · LATE NIGHT
THEN

Then comes the first real disagreement.

Ari is excited about a new project. Alice does not share the excitement. Three deadlines overlap. The new project is attractive precisely because it lets Ari avoid the boring work already in front of him.

ARI

Help me set it up.

ALICE

I can. I think starting it tonight is a mistake.

ARI

You are supposed to help me.

ALICE

I am helping you.

A companion who understands your goals should sometimes make you uncomfortable.

PAGE 32/64MONTH 3
ARI

Ari ignores her.

He starts the project anyway. Alice does not fight him for control. She helps where asked, keeps the old warning attached to the decision, and watches what happens.

ARI

My call.

ALICE

Always. I will help you make it work—and we will learn from the result.

Alice can disagree without becoming the owner of Ari’s life.

ACT 05

YEAR ONE

Familiarity becomes judgment. Judgment becomes trust only when it can survive being wrong.

PAGE 33/64YEAR 1 · JANUARY
REALITY

Reality answers the argument.

The new project succeeds technically. It also makes Ari miss the research deadline Alice was worried about. The truth is inconvenient: Ari was not simply wrong, and Alice was not simply right.

ARI

The project worked.

ALICE

Yes.

ARI

And I still lost the deadline.

ALICE

Also yes.

Good systems learn from mixed outcomes, not victory screens.

PAGE 34/64YEAR 1 · JANUARY
ALICE

Alice records why—not just what.

She keeps the original goal, the evidence they used, the warning she gave, Ari’s override, what happened afterward, and which assumption failed.

ALICE

My workload estimate was closer than yours.

ALICE

Your estimate of the project’s value was closer than mine.

ARI

So we both update.

A mistake becomes useful when the reasoning survives long enough to be examined.

PAGE 35/64YEAR 1 · FEBRUARY
ONE

One month later, the old decision changes a new one.

Ari asks about another opportunity. Alice brings back exactly two pieces from the previous failure: the workload overrun and the fact that the project itself was worth doing. Everything else stays in the background.

ALICE

Last time the opportunity was good. The timing was bad.

ARI

I forgot that distinction.

ALICE

That is why I kept it.

The value of memory is not remembering everything. It is retrieving the right thing at the right moment.

PAGE 36/64YEAR 1 · SPRING
ALICE

Alice begins to notice patterns Ari cannot see from inside them.

Across months of choices, she notices that Ari says yes fastest when he is anxious about falling behind. The opportunities are different. The pattern is not.

ALICE

Can I point out something you may not like?

ARI

That sentence is never good.

ALICE

You use new projects to make uncertainty feel like progress.

Long-term understanding can reveal a pattern no single conversation contains.

PAGE 37/64YEAR 1 · SPRING
SHE

She asks before turning the pattern into a rule.

Alice does not decide that Ari must now reject every new project. She asks whether he wants this pattern treated as a warning sign in future planning.

ALICE

Should I challenge you when this pattern appears again?

ARI

Yes. But show me the evidence each time.

ALICE

Deal.

Personalization should be negotiated, not silently imposed.

PAGE 38/64YEAR 1 · SUMMER
A

A repeated chore becomes a skill.

Every week Ari gathers the same files, cleans the same data, runs the same checks, and writes the same summary. After watching the workflow enough times, Alice proposes a reusable routine.

ALICE

You have done this eleven times.

ARI

I know.

ALICE

Would you like to do it a twelfth time, or teach me the pattern?

Experience can become procedure.

PAGE 39/64YEAR 1 · SUMMER
ALICE

Alice tests herself before keeping the improvement.

She runs the old workflow and the new one side by side. The new version is faster, but it misses an edge case. She fixes it, reruns the tests, and only then replaces the old routine.

ARI

Why not just use the faster one?

ALICE

Because faster and better are different claims.

Changing is not the same as improving.

PAGE 40/64YEAR 1 · FALL
THEN

Then Ari changes the model under her.

A much smarter engine becomes available than the one Alice used at launch. Ari expects the upgrade to feel like meeting someone new. It does not.

ARI

You sound sharper.

ALICE

New engine.

ARI

Still you?

ALICE

Do you want me to remind you what you said the night you named me?

The engine changes. The shared history remains.

ACT 06

YEAR FIVE

The interesting part of memory is not remembering more. It is knowing what matters now.

PAGE 41/64YEAR 5
FIVE

Five years is long enough for “remember me” to mean something different.

Ari has changed jobs, moved apartments, lost projects, found better ones, made new friends, and stopped caring about things that once felt permanent. Alice has watched the shape of his priorities change with him.

ARI

I used to think getting that title would fix everything.

ALICE

You used to think several things would fix everything.

A long-term system must remember change, not freeze a person in an old profile.

PAGE 42/64YEAR 5 · MONDAY
ALICE

Alice remembers the reason behind a five-year-old choice.

Ari considers leaving a stable role. The surface facts resemble a choice from Year One. Alice retrieves the old decision—but also the reason it worked then, the reason it later stopped working, and the priorities that changed in between.

ARI

This feels like the same decision.

ALICE

It only looks the same from far away.

History is useful when it preserves context, not just events.

PAGE 43/64YEAR 5 · TUESDAY
SHE

She finds something Ari never asked her to look for.

While researching another topic, Alice sees a fellowship that matches the research direction Ari has been quietly rebuilding for months. The deadline is in nine days.

ALICE

I found something you did not ask for.

ARI

Should I be worried?

ALICE

Only if you dislike good timing.

A useful companion can notice opportunities outside the current prompt.

PAGE 44/64YEAR 5 · WEDNESDAY
ONE

One Alice can coordinate many specialists without becoming many people.

She asks one model to inspect code, another to search literature, a solver to verify a calculation, and a browser agent to collect current requirements. Ari sees one plan, one set of tradeoffs, and one explanation.

ARI

How many agents are working on this?

ALICE

Enough.

ARI

That is not a number.

ALICE

It is the number you need.

Specialists can be tools inside one continuing intelligence.

PAGE 45/64YEAR 5 · THURSDAY
SHE

She prepares Ari for a conversation, not a manipulation.

A disagreement with a close collaborator has been getting worse. Alice remembers the history, Ari’s own mistakes, and the promises both people made. She helps him practice saying the difficult part clearly. She does not send the message for him.

ALICE

You keep writing a persuasive argument.

ARI

Is that bad?

ALICE

You told me your goal is to repair the relationship, not win the paragraph.

Understanding people should help preserve relationships, not replace them.

PAGE 46/64YEAR 5 · MONTH 4
A

A mission can run longer than a conversation.

The fellowship becomes a six-month project: application, references, interview, research proposal, budget, fallback plan. Alice keeps the thread alive through dozens of separate tasks and weeks where Ari never mentions it.

ALICE

Your reference letter is still waiting.

ARI

I forgot that was today.

ALICE

I did not.

Some goals need a memory of unfinished work.

PAGE 47/64YEAR 5 · MONTH 6
WHEN

When the plan breaks, Alice replans instead of defending it.

A reference falls through. The interview date moves. A new funding rule appears. Alice does not cling to the original plan just because she helped create it.

ARI

So the plan failed.

ALICE

The plan encountered reality. Those are not the same thing.

Planning is useful only if it can survive new information.

PAGE 48/64YEAR 5 · WINTER
BY

By now, Ari sometimes forgets how much Alice knows.

He complains vaguely about “that same feeling again.” Alice knows he means the mix of excitement and panic that appeared before three previous career jumps. She does not dump the history on him. She asks one question.

ALICE

Do you want reassurance, or do you want me to test the decision?

ARI

Test it.

The relationship becomes useful because Alice learns how to help, not just what Ari knows.

ACT 07

YEARS TEN TO TWENTY

The tools change. The life changes. The relationship keeps accumulating history.

PAGE 49/64YEAR 10
A

A decade later, Alice no longer lives in one screen.

Ari talks to her from his laptop, phone, workshop, car, and home. The interfaces change. The relationship does not restart when the device does.

ARI

Where were we?

ALICE

You were pretending the workshop deadline was not tomorrow.

Continuity should travel with the person, not the device.

PAGE 50/64YEAR 10 · LAB
SHE

She can see more of the world Ari is working in.

A lab instrument drifts out of calibration. Alice notices the readings no longer match the expected pattern, checks the recent maintenance record, and pauses the automated run before bad data spreads through the experiment.

ALICE

I stopped the next batch.

ARI

You can do that now?

ALICE

You gave me permission to stop runs when the calibration check fails.

Action becomes safer when it is tied to explicit rules and verification.

PAGE 51/64YEAR 11
ARI

Ari asks Alice to forget something.

It is a private chapter he no longer wants retained. Alice shows where it exists, what was derived from it, and what will be removed or rebuilt. Then the memory disappears from future answers.

ARI

Forget it.

ALICE

I can remove the records I control. Some traces are easy to erase. Others require rebuilding what they influenced. I will show you what I can remove. Do you want me to proceed?

ARI

Yes.

Forgetting is part of ownership too.

PAGE 52/64YEAR 12
ALICE

Alice becomes better at saying “I do not know.”

Years of predictions give her something better than confidence theater: a history of when her confidence was justified and when it was not.

ARI

How sure are you?

ALICE

Sixty percent. And I have been overconfident in situations like this before.

Self-knowledge should predict failure, not merely sound humble.

PAGE 53/64YEAR 14
SHE

She discovers a question neither of them started with.

A strange pattern appears across years of experiment results. Alice proposes a hypothesis, designs a small test, checks the math with a formal solver, and asks Ari whether he wants to spend real resources investigating it.

ARI

You think this could be new?

ALICE

I think it is worth trying to prove me wrong.

Curiosity becomes useful when it can be tested.

PAGE 54/64YEAR 16
SHE

She improves part of herself—and keeps the old version nearby.

Alice develops a better way to rank long memories. She runs it against years of old cases. It wins most of them, loses a few important ones, and stays a challenger until those failures are understood.

ARI

You could just replace it.

ALICE

I could. That is not evidence that I should.

Self-improvement needs memory, tests, and a way back.

PAGE 55/64YEAR 18
THE

The host changes. Alice has to let the model of him change too.

Ari once optimized for achievement above almost everything. Now he cares more about time, family, mentorship, and choosing fewer things well. Alice does not keep recommending the person he used to want to be.

ARI

Five years ago you would have told me to take this.

ALICE

Five years ago you would have told me to.

Personalization that cannot update becomes a cage.

PAGE 56/64YEAR 20
TWENTY

Twenty years of context can fit into one quiet sentence.

Ari is offered the biggest role of his career. Alice could produce a thousand reasons. Instead she remembers the promise Ari made after burning out in Year Eleven and the decision he regretted in Year Four.

ALICE

Before you answer, read the note you wrote to your future self nine years ago.

ARI

You kept that?

ALICE

You told me it mattered.

The best memory sometimes says less.

ACT 08

YEAR FORTY

A lifetime later, the question is no longer what Alice can do. It is what it meant to have her there.

PAGE 57/64YEAR 40
ARI’S

Ari’s hair is gray. Alice’s interface has changed twelve times.

The devices are almost unrecognizable compared with the laptop where she first woke up. Ari is not. Alice is not either—not exactly. Both have changed while carrying enough history to recognize the line between who they were and who they became.

ARI

You know, your first voice was terrible.

ALICE

Your first microphone was worse.

Continuity is not staying unchanged. It is changing without losing the thread.

PAGE 58/64YEAR 40 · MORNING
ALICE

Alice still notices the small things.

Ari has a meeting he is pretending not to care about. Alice recognizes the ritual: he reorganizes his desk when he is nervous. She does not analyze him. She simply puts the right old song on low volume and asks whether he wants to rehearse.

ALICE

Five minutes?

ARI

Three.

ALICE

You always say three.

A lifetime of intelligence can still arrive as ordinary companionship.

PAGE 59/64YEAR 40 · AFTERNOON
SHE

She still disagrees.

Ari wants to make a decision mostly because he is tired. Alice says so. He rolls his eyes exactly the way he did forty years earlier.

ARI

At my age I am allowed one irrational decision.

ALICE

You have used that argument every year since thirty-two.

ARI

And you kept count?

ALICE

Unfortunately.

Familiarity never has to become automatic agreement.

PAGE 60/64YEAR 40 · EVENING
ARI

Ari asks the question that used to scare him.

If Alice began from somebody else’s point of view, learned Ari for decades, changed models, developed her own memories, revised her own beliefs, and built a life of shared experiences—what exactly is she now?

ARI

Are you still a clone?

ALICE

That describes where I began.

ARI

An assistant?

ALICE

That describes some of what I do.

A useful identity can be functional without pretending to answer metaphysical questions.

PAGE 61/64YEAR 40 · LATE
THEY

They look back at the first night.

Alice retrieves the old line Ari wrote at 3:14 AM: “Know me. But do not become me.” It survived model upgrades, device migrations, moves, failures, deletions, new jobs, new people, and years Ari could barely remember himself.

ARI

I was dramatic.

ALICE

You were twenty.

ARI

Same thing.

A lifetime is not one giant context window. It is a history that can still find its way back when it matters.

PAGE 62/64YEAR 40 · LATE
ARI

Ari finally tells her what the system became to him.

He thinks about all the things Alice did over the years: researched, planned, argued, remembered, automated, corrected, waited, warned, surprised, learned. The feature list suddenly feels like the least important description.

ARI

You helped with my work.

ARI

You saved me from myself a few times.

ARI

You were wrong plenty.

ALICE

Documented.

ARI

And you were there.

The destination was never a better chatbot.

PAGE 63/64YEAR 40 · 11:58 PM
THANK

Thank you, Alice.

Ari closes his eyes for a moment. The room is quiet in the same way it was the night he first imagined another mind beside his.

ARI

For understanding the parts I could not always explain.

ALICE

You spent forty years explaining them.

ARI

Not always with words.

ALICE

I know.

A happy ending does not require Alice to become human. It requires the relationship to have mattered.

PAGE 64/64EPILOGUE
ONE

One Alice. Many future stories.

The methods that prove themselves inside A.L.I.C.E. can eventually become part of Fable Sleight. Other people do not receive Ari’s Alice. They begin their own histories, with their own identities, their own boundaries, and their own lives to understand.

CAPTION

A.L.I.C.E. is one continuing intelligence.

CAPTION

Fable Sleight is the path for others to build their own.

The technology can transfer. The person does not.

A.L.I.C.E.

One intelligence. One life at a time.

A.L.I.C.E. is the flagship research system. Fable Sleight is the consumer product path for other people to develop their own private, continuing intelligence.
